## Research focus
This area studies strategic decisions in family-owned businesses through decision governance, interdependencies and scenario resilience.

## Why family-business decisions require a separate lens
In a family-owned company, a strategic decision can affect several systems at the same time: ownership, operating control, family governance, succession, capital structure, tax and regulatory exposure, reputation and relationships between family branches. A decision that appears attractive in one dimension may create fragility in another.

Decision Security therefore treats the family enterprise not only as a company, but as a connected decision system in which business and ownership consequences must be assessed together.

## High-stakes decision triggers
The research program gives particular attention to decisions that are difficult or costly to reverse, including:

- succession and transfer of control;
- sale of the business or a significant equity stake;
- acquisitions and major capital expenditure;
- entry into a new country, market or regulated sector;
- restructuring of ownership or a family holding structure;
- relocation or change of jurisdiction;
- creation or redesign of a family office;
- major digital or AI transformation;
- decisions with material regulatory, reputational or family-conflict exposure.

## Decision Security perspective
The core research question is not simply whether a proposed transaction or strategy is legally possible or financially attractive. It is whether the decision remains defensible when its assumptions, evidence, dependencies, alternative scenarios and implementation conditions are examined together.

TAMVER separates independent decision verification from execution. Legal, tax, investment and transaction advisers may remain responsible for implementation. The Decision Security layer examines the decision before commitment and makes conflicts, dependencies and unresolved assumptions visible to the family and its governing bodies.

## Research dimensions
Family-business cases are examined through six recurring dimensions:

1. **Irreversibility** — how difficult and costly it would be to reverse the decision.
2. **Capital exposure** — how much family wealth or operating capacity is placed at risk.
3. **Family-governance exposure** — whether different branches, generations or control rights are affected.
4. **Regulatory and cross-border exposure** — whether the decision depends on tax, AML, substance, reporting or jurisdictional conditions.
5. **Adviser incentives** — whether advisers involved in execution also benefit from the transaction proceeding.
6. **Reputational and operating exposure** — whether the decision can affect continuity, trust, market position or critical operating dependencies.

## Typical TAMVER methods
Depending on the decision, the research layer may combine:

- DSS to examine decision quality, evidence and implementation readiness;
- AIR to map cross-system and cross-sector dependencies;
- Scenario Stress-Test to test alternative futures and failure conditions;
- SCM to examine the resilience of the structure expected to carry the decision;
- Evidence Registry logic to keep source-backed facts separate from assumptions, calculations and expert interpretation.
