## Objective
The audit creates a traceable view of the assumptions, evidence, dependencies and implementation risks supporting a strategic decision.

## What the audit is for
Strategic Decision Audit is designed for high-stakes decisions made before an irreversible or expensive commitment. It does not replace legal due diligence, tax structuring, investment advice or transaction execution. Its role is to test whether the decision itself is sufficiently supported, resilient and executable.

Typical applications include a business sale, acquisition, major investment, cross-border expansion, restructuring, relocation, strategic technology program or another decision where the cost of a wrong commitment is material.

## Core audit questions
The audit examines six questions:

1. **What decision is actually being made?** The object, mandate, horizon, decision owner and success conditions must be explicit.
2. **Which assumptions carry the decision?** Critical assumptions are identified and separated from verified facts.
3. **What evidence supports the decision?** Evidence quality, traceability, freshness and areas of disagreement are reviewed.
4. **Which dependencies can change the outcome?** AIR is used where sector, stakeholder, resource, regulatory or infrastructure relationships can propagate risk.
5. **Does the decision survive alternative scenarios?** Material shocks, critical points and failure conditions are tested through scenario analysis.
6. **Can the organisation implement and govern it?** Implementation readiness, accountability, monitoring and adaptation capacity are reviewed before a Decision Gate is formed.

## Standard analytical sequence
A full audit can combine the following stages:

- scoping of the decision and decision boundary;
- evidence collection and source classification;
- interviews with decision owners and relevant functional experts;
- DSS assessment of maturity, risk exposure, confidence and implementation readiness;
- AIR mapping of material interdependencies;
- Scenario Stress-Test of alternative conditions and critical points;
- expert validation and identification of unresolved evidence gaps;
- Decision Gate and priority roadmap.

## Outputs
Depending on scope, the audit can produce a traceable package containing:

- decision definition and assumption register;
- DSS domain assessment and Decision Security view;
- evidence-confidence findings;
- AIR critical-link and dependency map;
- scenario pressure and critical-point findings;
- implementation and governance gaps;
- Decision Gate conditions;
- prioritised actions and monitoring requirements.

## Decision Gate principle
The output is not a promise that risk has been eliminated. The Decision Gate records whether the current decision can proceed, should proceed only under explicit conditions, requires redesign or lacks sufficient evidence for commitment.

## Independence boundary
TAMVER's Decision Security role is analytically separate from advisers whose remuneration may depend on execution, assets under management, product placement or transaction completion. Existing legal, tax, financial and operating advisers can remain part of the process while the decision is independently tested.

## Publication rule
Client-specific findings and quantitative conclusions remain non-public unless explicitly approved through the TAMVER publication process. Public claims should remain traceable to validated evidence rather than being inferred from a completed internal assessment.
