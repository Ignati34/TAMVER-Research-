## Research agenda
The program develops concepts, methods and comparative research around strategic decision resilience.

## Purpose
The Decision Security Research Program develops the analytical basis for examining whether high-stakes strategic decisions are evidence-based, resilient, executable and governable before commitment.

The program is not limited to a single sector. It studies decision systems across governments, family businesses, institutions, investment programs, infrastructure and complex corporate environments where multiple dependencies interact.

## Core research domains
The program is organised around five connected domains:

1. **Decision quality** — how strategic decisions are framed, governed and tested before commitment.
2. **Evidence quality** — how source strength, traceability, freshness, uncertainty and expert disagreement affect confidence in a decision.
3. **Interdependencies** — how sector, stakeholder, resource, regulatory and technological relationships propagate risk and opportunity.
4. **Scenario resilience** — how a decision behaves under alternative futures, shocks, critical points and changing assumptions.
5. **Structural resilience** — whether the organisation, system or jurisdiction expected to carry the decision has sufficient operational, institutional and resource capacity.

## Methodological architecture
The research program connects the principal TAMVER analytical layers:

- DSS provides the core Decision Security assessment framework;
- AIR maps interdependencies, cascading effects, critical points and opportunity pathways;
- SCM examines structural resilience across key security dimensions;
- Scenario Stress-Test tests alternative conditions and failure paths;
- Evidence Registry separates source-backed facts from assumptions, calculations and expert interpretation.

These layers are intended to be read together rather than as isolated scores.

## Research outputs
The program can support:

- methodology papers and controlled definitions;
- sector and country research;
- comparative Decision Security studies;
- evidence-backed case analyses;
- benchmark development;
- scenario libraries and early-warning frameworks;
- public research briefs and executive reports;
- refinement of assessment instruments and digital analytical tools.

## Evidence and provenance rule
The Research layer does not create a parallel evidence system. Public analytical claims should remain traceable through the TAMVER evidence architecture. Validation does not automatically mean publication: evidence, metrics and claims must pass the relevant publication controls before appearing in public research.

## Research discipline
The program distinguishes four different objects that must not be collapsed into one another:

- verified external facts;
- internal assumptions and model inputs;
- calculated outputs;
- expert interpretation.

This separation is essential for preserving the difference between what is known, what is modelled and what is concluded.

## Development direction
The long-term objective is to turn accumulated research, validated evidence, scenario analysis and comparative cases into a reusable Decision Security knowledge system that can support both human review and digital decision-support tools without weakening provenance or publication controls.
